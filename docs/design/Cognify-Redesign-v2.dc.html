Can you explain how neural networks actually learn? I'm an intermediate CS student, so go a level deeper than the textbook intro.
MH

A network learns by measuring how wrong it is, then nudging every weight in the direction that reduces that error. Three moving parts make it work:

1 Forward pass — inputs flow through the layers and produce a prediction.
2 Loss — a function scores the gap between prediction and truth.
3 Backprop — gradients flow backward and each weight shifts a little.
Intuition
Think of it as walking downhill in fog — the gradient is the slope under your feet, and the learning rate is how big a step you take.
